### 2. Please describe your approach in detail (how you went from variant/mechanism to candidate medication(s)).
Overview. The approach goes from the variant mechanism found in Track 1 to five consequences, to a verified literature layer, to computational lines that each propose candidates, to computational refutation checks and to a laboratory plan. Scope: approved FDA drugs only; every candidate is a hypothesis for follow-up, not evidence of efficacy; the child's age is unknown and never assumed; no dose, exposure or treatment recommendation is given.

1. Variant mechanism. The only patient input is a gene-level, categorical extract of the Track 1 result (seven genes, no positions, alleles or calls). BUB1B carries a stop-gain (p.Leu737Ter) with predicted nonsense-mediated decay and a missense variant of uncertain significance (p.Asn1002Lys) in the kinase-like domain, phase unresolved. Under rules written beforehand, BUB1B is the only causal candidate and IGBP1 is a hypothesis-grade modifier.

2. Five consequences of reduced BubR1 function, tied to the child's features: mosaic aneuploidy; proteotoxic and energy stress; p53 and p16 senescence; growth restriction and muscle loss; tumour predisposition. Mosaic aneuploidy in the child is not shown; a karyotype or single-cell sequencing is the first test.

3. Literature layer with verification. Eight AI-assisted searches (675 queries): four by consequence (419 queries) and four drug-agnostic (256 queries, run because the first wording steered toward the final drugs). Each sentence carries an evidence level: L0 inferred; L1 related system; L2 BUB1B- or MVA-derived human cells or hypomorph with rescue; L3 clinical observation in MVA or BUB1B. Code re-fetched every quoted sentence from Europe PMC: of 363 sentence slots, 355 verified, 6 preprint sentences not checkable (not cited), 2 not found (excluded). Selection rules R1 to R4 (R1 the drug is approved; R2 constitutional hypothesis needs benefit at L1 or more on a feature of the child, tumour-context hypothesis needs a verified L1 finding in an aneuploid or tumour system; R3 a class sibling is listed with its representative; R4 an L3 harm or flagged finding goes to the caution list) were declared after the literature was read and are labelled post hoc. Result: nine drugs in three groups. Constitutional: sirolimus, everolimus (class sibling), metformin, sodium phenylbutyrate. Tumour context: trametinib, selumetinib (class sibling). Hazards to check, not proposed: bortezomib, chloroquine, bosutinib. Best level L1; none reaches L2 or L3. The second round found no approved drug that rescues a BubR1-deficient phenotype.

4. Four computational lines plus signature reversal, each proposing candidates by its own native criterion. Line A: knowledge graph (PrimeKG personalised PageRank, side-effect edges removed). Line B: network proximity on STRING v12.0 physical interactions (B1 phenotype module, B2 BUB1B module). Line C: cell-line vulnerability (DepMap and PRISM 24Q2 against a checkpoint-weakness score; later adjusted for lineage and proliferation: of 23 original hits, 6 survive). Line D: signed direction on OmniPath. Line E: signature reversal in LINCS L1000 libraries, for a BUB1B knockout signature (0 usable drugs) and a patient signature from public series GSE22206 (18 usable drugs, post hoc). A frozen literature-grounded evidence engine is also applied. The protocol rule (at least two usable lines among A, B, C) yields no candidate. Candidates are therefore reported per line, not as one ranking: 6 literature, 10 engine, 2 graph, 3 proximity B2, 5 cell-line, 5 direction, 5 patient-signature (line E, patient signature); none from B1, from the knockout signature or from the combined Track 1 signature.

5. Candidates proposed by each line with age-band label readings. For each candidate the table gives the native score, the evidence tier, the label reading in bands 0-4 / 5-11 / 12-17 years (L labelled, P partly, N not labelled, V verify, A adults only; read from label text by an AI assistant, not a pharmacist), safety flags and checks. 28 distinct drugs appear across the tables and 7 have label support in at least one band. Convergence is information only: neratinib (cell-line and patient-signature) and trametinib (literature and cell-line) are the only drugs in two lines, each pair including a weak line.

6. Multi-parameter cross-check. A stochastic multicriteria acceptability analysis (10,000 weight draws, 11 criteria, seed 20261018), fixed before computing, orders hypotheses and creates no evidence. Run 1 (pool of 84) proposed trametinib (probability in the top five 0.50); run 2 (pool of 224 approved small molecules after approval checks) proposed calcitriol (0.74), read as an artefact of missing-data renormalisation and of a label hazard (nephrocalcinosis term in its contraindications), and calcitriol is not a candidate.

7. Computational refutation checks CF1 to CF9 (computational follow-up checks, specified before any was run): CF1 sign audit of line C; CF2 tubulin-class enrichment among line C survivors (refuted, so line C behaves as a mitotic-dependency control); CF3 engine specificity by co-mention with BUB1B against 99 degree-matched genes (all five tested refuted); CF4 hub-free paths of length 4 or less in PrimeKG (permissive); CF5 sensitivity of the B2 module scheme; CF6 net signed effect against 1,000 sign-shuffled networks; CF7 six leave-one-array-out runs of the patient signature (five drugs refuted); CF8 STRING distance from IGBP1 to MTOR, PRKAA1 and PRKAA2 (refuted: the IGBP1 rationale is dropped); CF9 replication in an independent screen, GDSC2 (only paclitaxel estimable; not replicated). Outcome over 32 triaged rows: 16 closed as computational candidates; 7 still hypotheses that passed a check (lazertinib, ceritinib, selinexor, tirbanibulin, pomalidomide, disopyramide, nirogacestat); 5 literature drugs with no applicable check (sirolimus, everolimus, metformin, sodium phenylbutyrate, trametinib); 4 tubulin binders kept for toxicity design only.

8. Experiment plan with Step 0. Computation first, laboratory only for what computation cannot refute. Step 0 (E0): karyotype and single-cell sequencing of blood, the same for the megabase-scale events, a missense function test, and phase by parental or long-read testing, with a stop rule. Then M1 (cell models: an isogenic partial-BUB1B-reduction line in two clones, euploid controls, rescue), then X1 (sirolimus, everolimus, metformin), X2 (sodium phenylbutyrate), X3 (trametinib in tumour lines), X4 (lazertinib, neratinib), X5 (ceritinib, selinexor, tirbanibulin) and H1 (harm panel). Sample sizes are planning numbers from a power analysis. None of this has been run.

9. Scalability evidence. The evidence engine was validated on other rare diseases: pre-registered gain over a popularity baseline of 0.21 AUC (17 diseases) and 0.19 (42 sealed held-out diseases of 145). Its edge at low literature volume comes from the knowledge-graph prior, and the per-pair language-model reading test was not informative. Applied to MVA and Fanconi anaemia it yields no candidate list. The pipeline reran byte for byte on two synthetic cases (a Fanconi anaemia type and an MVA type).

### 9. How did you characterize the variant's mechanism (e.g., loss-of-function vs. gain-of-function, pathway disrupted, downstream biological consequence)?
Loss versus gain of function. The stop-gain p.Leu737Ter lies in exon 17 of 23, 746 nucleotides upstream of the last exon-exon junction, beyond the 50-55 nucleotide rule, so nonsense-mediated decay is predicted (not tested). Any product would lack residues 737-1050 (30 % of the protein), including the kinase-like domain: loss of function. The missense variant p.Asn1002Lys is of uncertain significance. Asn is conserved in 116 of 120 aligned orthologs (window identity 0.78); the residue lies inside the kinase-like domain and is buried in the AlphaFold model (pLDDT 91.06; relative accessible surface at the 8.0th percentile of Asn; 27 residues within 8 angstrom); the nearest known pathogenic missense is 10 residues away; a ClinVar window of plus or minus 20 residues holds 0 pathogenic or likely pathogenic against 2 benign or likely benign, so there is no regional signal. Predictors disagree: AlphaMissense 0.923 (moderate) and PolyPhen-2 0.997 (supporting) give computational evidence, REVEL 0.472, CADD 24.5 and SIFT 0.01 give none, and the strengths are not added. Working hypothesis: partial loss of function (hypomorph); there is no evidence of gain of function.

Phase. Unresolved. As an explicit inference, the posterior for the two variants being in trans is 0.999999 under stated assumptions (prior 0.5, penetrance 0.9, alternative-cause probability 1e-6), and it falls to 0.5 if BUB1B is not causal; parental testing or long-read sequencing would decide. The analysis keeps complete loss and hypomorph as two direction scenarios.

Pathway. BubR1 belongs to the mitotic checkpoint complex of the spindle assembly checkpoint; reduced function lets anaphase start before all kinetochores are attached.

Five consequences tied to the child: mosaic aneuploidy; proteotoxic and energy stress; p53 and p16 senescence; growth restriction and muscle loss; tumour predisposition (levels reached: mosaic aneuploidy L2, tumour predisposition L3, on the scale L0 inferred to L3 clinical observation). Mosaic aneuploidy in the child is not shown: a coarse read-depth screen cannot detect low-fraction mosaicism and is not a karyotype. Nephrocalcinosis and the parental miscarriages are not linked to BUB1B in any retrieved paper.

Differential diagnosis against curated MVA genes. Seven Track 1 genes were classified by rules written beforehand (curated MVA gene, genotype versus inheritance, consequence class, phenotype fit, ClinVar): BUB1B causal candidate (phenotype score 0.557, rank 14); MAD1L1 contextual; CENPE, DNAH10, IGBP1, IZUMO4 and ZNF418 insufficient evidence. The MVA gene landscape holds 7 curated and 3 proposed genes, and only BUB1B and MAD1L1 appear in Track 1.

Track 1 gene block. A gene block with a weight cap of 0.1 for modifiers keeps IGBP1 (hypothesis-grade modifier; no function test, and a network-distance rationale to the mTOR and AMPK axis was refuted), CENPE and MAD1L1 (context), and CDC20, MAD2L1BP, TTK and CEP192 (megabase-scale events, undetermined until a karyotype or single-cell sequencing).

Case profile. Compared with 31 published BUB1B cases, the child shows growth restriction and malignancy (rhabdomyosarcoma); frequent features such as microcephaly and developmental delay are unknown. This is a list of what to check, not a diagnosis.

### 11. Method abstract (up to 500 words)
Mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA1) is an ultrarare disorder of the spindle assembly checkpoint. The Track 1 analysis points to BUB1B: a stop-gain (p.Leu737Ter, exon 17 of 23, decay predicted) and a missense variant of uncertain significance (p.Asn1002Lys) in the kinase-like domain, phase unresolved. No approved drug restores BubR1. We asked which FDA-approved drugs are plausible candidates for further investigation. Every output is a hypothesis for follow-up, not evidence of efficacy; no dose or treatment is recommended, and the child's age is unknown and never assumed.

Approach. (1) Mechanism: reduced BubR1 function has five consequences tied to the child's features: mosaic aneuploidy, proteotoxic and energy stress, p53 and p16 senescence, growth restriction and muscle loss, tumour predisposition. (2) A drug-agnostic literature layer: eight AI-assisted searches (675 queries) collected sentences about approved drugs; code re-fetched each from Europe PMC (355 of 363 slots verified; 2 not found, excluded). Selection rules, declared after the literature was read, gave nine drugs in three groups. The best evidence level is L1 (a related system); none reaches L2 or L3. (3) Computational lines, each proposing candidates by its own criterion: knowledge graph (line A), network proximity (B1 phenotype module, B2 BUB1B module), cell-line vulnerability (line C, adjusted for lineage and proliferation), signed direction (line D), signature reversal in LINCS libraries (line E), and a frozen literature engine. The protocol rule of two usable lines yields no candidate, so candidates are reported per line with age-band label readings and safety flags, and convergence is information only: 28 distinct drugs, two of them in two lines. A multi-parameter cross-check (10,000 weight draws) orders hypotheses and creates no evidence. (4) Nine computational follow-ups (CF1 to CF9) tried to refute the candidates: 16 of 32 triaged candidates were closed; seven remain hypotheses that passed a check, beside five literature drugs and four tubulin binders kept as hazard class. (5) A laboratory plan starts with Step 0 (karyotype and single-cell sequencing, missense function test, phase testing); no laboratory work has been done.

Scalability. The evidence engine beat a popularity baseline by AUC 0.21 on 17 pre-registered rare diseases and by 0.19 on 42 sealed held-out diseases; the pipeline reran byte for byte on two synthetic cases.

Strengths. Rules and thresholds were written before the runs where marked; every quoted sentence is checked by code; negative results are reported (the graph ranking is generic: 43 of 46 top drugs also rank for random queries; line C falls from 22 to 5 hits after covariates; the one independent replication failed); all 71 drugs of the study are confirmed in Drugs@FDA.

Limitations. One case; mosaicism, phase and the missense effect are untested; evidence is indirect (for sirolimus, 3 benefit and 11 harm sentences); the search queries were not independent of the final drugs; the engine's edge at low literature volume comes from the knowledge-graph prior, not from the literature; label and evidence readings are AI readings without specialist review; the order of protocol before results rests on the author's statement, with no time stamp.
